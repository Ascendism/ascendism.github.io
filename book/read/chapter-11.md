# Chapter 11 — Use the Window Without Becoming Owned

Law, export rules, pricing, terms of service, and concentration narrow access from the outside. This chapter names what happens inside your week while those clocks tick—attention, habit, account security, and the difference between a tool you deploy and a feed that deploys you.

Owned is not a mood. It is a structure: defaults you never chose, sessions that outlive the task, credentials scattered across vendors, skills that thin because the interface always offers the next completion, and a body trained to check before the body trained to finish the work. The window stays useful only if you can walk through it and still recognize your hands on the other side.

---

Loops are designed. Average effects are small. Both can be true.

Screen-time panic already ran its course in public debate. The peer literature matters here for a different reason—not to declare phones harmless, but to separate mechanism from headline.

Orben and Przybylski's time-use-diary work in *Psychological Science* (2019; verify effect sizes and wording at publication) finds associations between digital technology use and adolescent psychological well-being that are small relative to many other life variables in the same datasets. Large samples. Careful measurement. A result that should humble anyone writing a sermon about "destroyed generations."

That is true. It does not follow that retention design is imaginary.

A social feed and a field notebook can both glow on glass. They do not share a business model. One sells return visits priced in attention and data; the other sells nothing while you write unless you later attach a subscription. The small average effect in population studies does not erase the engineering fact that infinite scroll, variable reward schedules, and notification stacks exist because they increase time-on-platform. It means moral panic is a blunt instrument—and blunt instruments get wielded at the wrong target while the design keeps shipping.

WHO screen-time guidance for children (re-pull the current age brackets at publication) is worth citing when you touch parenting: the organization distinguishes sedentary screen time from physical activity and sleep displacement—not "screens evil, period." Use clinical addiction language sparingly. Pathologizing an ordinary reader's Tuesday night check-in is not care. Describing a product built to maximize sessions is not denial.

Your design question is sharper than "how many hours": who owns the interrupt? If the tool can ping you but you cannot silence it without fighting settings buried three menus deep, the ownership line has already moved.

---

Digital minimalism is a foil, not an enemy.

Cal Newport's *Digital Minimalism* and related "deep work" writing gave millions of people permission to say no to default stacks. The steelman: modern knowledge work fragments by design; protecting blocks of uninterrupted attention is a legitimate craft move, not Luddism. A welder does not take calls inside the spark zone. A grazier on fence repair does not owe the group chat a minute-by-minute photo stream. A municipal engineer reviewing a heat-recovery sketch needs the same protected hour—even if the "distraction" is another tab, not a phone buzz.

The strawman to refuse: minimalism as identity purity—the person who brags about no smartphone while outsourcing every coordination friction to someone else's device and labor. Or minimalism as one-size austerity that treats assistive tech as optional luxury. Reducing noise is not the same as reducing capability.

Minimalism fails as a universal prescription when it treats all digital contact as fungible. Your co-op's shared spreadsheet is not your cousin's outrage loop. A local model on a laptop is not a casino feed. The filter is not "less screen." The filter is whether this object serves the target you named—autonomy, land, resilience, paid work—or rents your nervous system to someone else's metric.

Practical middle: protocols, not vibes. A weekly "office hours" window for messages. A phone drawer during one physical task. A browser profile that cannot open social sites during drafting. A grazing log on paper first, photo second—because the thumb wants to edit before the eye has counted. None of that requires pretending the peer literature says catastrophe. It requires admitting you are fighting product managers, not weak will.

---

Skills maintenance is maintenance, not nostalgia.

Ericsson-style deliberate practice is easy to misquote into "ten thousand hours" folklore. Light touch only: skills decay when feedback loops shorten. If the tool always drafts, you stop drafting; if the map always routes, you stop reading terrain; if the model always summarizes the ordinance PDF, you stop noticing which clause actually binds the setback.

Trade craft makes the point without romance. An apprentice electrician still turns screws under supervision after the power-tool arrives. The tool speeds the hand; it does not replace the code book in the pocket. AI-assisted desk work fails the same way when "good enough" output ships without a human who can spot a wrong unit, a wrong species in a planting plan, or a wrong assumption about who holds the easement.

Place-first example: you use a model to draft soil-amendment rates from lab PDFs. The verification step is a spreadsheet you still understand—formulas visible, units checked against the lab's original table, one row spot-calculated by hand. Systems-first example: you use codegen to scaffold an API client. The verification step is one request replayed in curl with headers you typed, not only a green test badge. Same rule: the window should not close over a skill you no longer possess when the vendor changes the UI.

---

Security hygiene is sovereignty infrastructure.

CISA's public guidance is boring on purpose—verify current URLs at publication. Password managers. Phishing recognition. Multi-factor authentication on email and cloud accounts that gate your manuscript, your co-op books, your domain registrar. Patched operating systems on the machine that holds keys. Backups you have restored once, not backups you intend to restore.

This is not paranoia. Terms of service are quiet enclosure; account takeover is the loud version: you lose the repo, the newsletter list, the grant login, the irrigation controller dashboard because one reused password sat in a breach dump from a forum you forgot you joined.

Steelman the vendor: hosted platforms ship patch cadence and physical security many small shops cannot replicate in a closet rack. True. It does not follow that your identity perimeter should be thin. Hygiene shifts the asymmetry slightly back toward you without pretending you can out-engineer a nation-state.

A concrete stack sketch, with tradeoffs disclosed—not a product ad. Identity layer: password manager plus MFA on mail, DNS, bank, and cloud—costs an afternoon of setup and recovery codes stored offline. Work layer: offline-capable notes with periodic export—sync conflicts you resolve yourself. Inference layer: local model for drafts plus hosted model for tasks that need scale—two eval habits, two privacy postures. Comms layer: one channel for urgent, one for async—colleagues must learn your map. Diversifying tools is not hoarding. It is avoiding single-vendor capture of your attention, your files, and your inference path—enclosure experienced at human scale.

---

Local-first is a posture, not a purity badge.

The local-first software movement pushes data toward devices you control, with collaboration layered on top—cite specific tools you actually run and disclose sync and backup behavior at publication. The steelman: latency, privacy, and survivorship when the uplink dies mid-harvest week. The honest counter: conflict resolution, backup discipline, and the social cost of asking neighbors to install another app.

You do not owe the movement a religion. You owe yourself a test: if this service vanished tomorrow, what do you still have on disk? If the answer is "nothing," you are not using a window. You are renting a room with no furniture you built.

---

Name who may interrupt you, and when. Keep one skill verification step the tool cannot skip for you. Treat security basics as part of the build, like grounding a panel. Hold at least one offline copy of work that matters if the account locks.

The exit ramp comes next—land access, savings, formation, the slower clocks. The body and the account still belong to you while you use the machines.
