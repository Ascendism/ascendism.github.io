Last week’s piece on data-center tax clocks left one lever on the table. OBBBA moves money forward — bonus depreciation, wind/solar construction windows. Executive Order 14318, signed July 23, 2025, moves federal permitting and land forward for hyperscale AI load. Same sprint. Different paperwork.

Tax policy answers “why build now?” Permitting policy answers “why can you break ground before the county meeting finishes?”

What the order targets

EO 14318 is not about enterprise colocation or a 2 MW edge site. It defines a Data Center Project as new load above 100 megawatts for AI training, inference, simulation, or synthetic data generation. Transmission lines, substations, gas pipelines, backup power, and semiconductor supply chains sit in the “covered components” bucket.

Data Center Project
>100 MW new load for AI training, inference, simulation, or synthetic data
Qualifying Project
≥$500M capex, >100 MW load, national-security nexus, or agency strategic designation

Below that threshold, most of the federal machinery does not apply. Above it, the order reads like an infrastructure clearance lane.

What it actually does

The White House text and Federal Register notice (published July 28, 2025) bundle several moves:

  • NEPA streamlining — categorical exclusions and faster environmental review for qualifying AI infrastructure
  • Federal land — agencies directed to make suitable federal acreage available for data-center development
  • Financial support menu — loans, guarantees, grants, and tax incentives referenced alongside permitting
  • FAST-41 alignment — expedited permitting framework for covered projects
  • Frame swap — revokes Biden EO 14141; replaces a safety-first AI order with a deregulatory infrastructure frame tied to China competition

Steel-man both sides before you pick a lane. Proponents: if compute capacity is strategic, federal delay is a national liability. Critics: local environmental review and land-use leverage shrink when NEPA shortcuts and federal acreage enter the picture. Both can be true at once.

Two levers, one race

Read OBBBA and EO 14318 together. A hyperscaler racing a 2026–2027 wind/solar construction window also wants a permitting track that does not stall on a three-year EIS while transformers sit in queue. Tax clocks and permit clocks stack — they do not cancel each other.

Questions worth asking at a hearing

County boards and PUC dockets are where abstract orders become concrete. Useful questions:

  • Does this project exceed the 100 MW AI load threshold — or is the applicant bundling campus phases to qualify?
  • Is it designated a Qualifying Project under the order’s capex and strategic criteria?
  • Which NEPA track applies — full EIS, EA, or categorical exclusion?
  • Is any portion on federal land, and who holds the lease terms?
  • Who pays for transmission upgrades if the federal lane clears generation but the local utility queue does not?

The bottom line

Surveillance-center rumors miss the filing stack. OBBBA pulls depreciation and renewable construction forward. EO 14318 pulls federal review and land forward for >100 MW AI campuses. Follow both levers — and the interconnection queue behind them — if you want to know where the next wave lands before the comment thread does.
